Headway Cognitive Rehabilitation Tool · Product Roadmap

From session tracker
to clinical intelligence platform

Version1.3 — Phase 1 live
DateJuly 2026
AudienceHeadway leadership, key workers, clinical team
Prepared byDuncan Hendy

This roadmap describes a path from a working session tool to a network-wide clinical intelligence platform — built around how Headway key workers actually think and talk about their clients.

The foundation is already live. The architecture has been deliberately kept simple, robust, and free of external AI dependencies, so every improvement compounds on something stable.

Phases are ordered by the ratio of impact to difficulty. High-value, low-effort wins come first. The longer-horizon features are more ambitious — but by the time we reach them, we'll have the data, the trust, and the relationships to make them land properly.

Phase 1 · Live now

Deficit-led session setup — therapist picks the cognitive area, exercises are auto-assigned
Rule-based insight engine — plateau alerts, regression detection, domain analysis
Client profiles with domain radar charts
Session reports with per-exercise breakdowns
Offline-capable PWA — works in low-connectivity settings
Design principle across all phases: every feature must work for a Headway key worker at the end of a long shift, on a shared tablet, with a client who has limited patience for complexity. If it needs a training manual, we haven't finished it yet.
The roadmap

Six phases, ordered by impact-to-difficulty ratio. Each phase is independently useful — we're not waiting for the full picture to deliver value.

2
Goals, templates, export & home practice
Near-term

The missing layer between "session data" and "meaningful care record." Connecting exercises to goals makes progress legible to the client, the family, and the wider MDT. Home practice means rehabilitation doesn't stop when the session ends — and syncs back to the same record.

Goal setting per client (e.g. "return to independent cooking")
Link sessions to specific goals — show progress visually
Session plan templates by deficit profile (post-stroke, TBI, etc.)
PDF session report — one-click, print-ready for MDT folders
Per-exercise difficulty (override session-level setting)
Progress milestone celebrations for clients
Home practice mode — key worker prescribes domains and difficulty for independent use
Client access via PIN or magic link — no account, no password, time-limited token
Cross-platform home access — any Android or iOS device the client owns
Home sessions sync to database, tagged as "home practice" in the client profile
Key worker sees compliance — which home sessions were completed and when

3
Natural language intake + role-based access
Medium-term

Every Headway key worker describes a client differently. This phase makes the app understand all of them — plain language, charity terminology, or clinical shorthand. It also introduces a proper permission model: every role sees exactly what they need, nothing more.

Free-text intake: describe the client's difficulties in plain words
AI maps description to cognitive domains + suggests session plan
Works with any terminology — lay, clinical, or charity-specific
Key worker reviews and adjusts before the suggestion is accepted
Session notes search, tagging, and flagging
AI always has a coded fallback — never a blank screen
Client sharing — key worker can share specific clients with a named colleague for cover, handover, or co-working
Shared access is per-client, time-limited or permanent, and revocable — colleague sees only those clients, nothing else

Role-based access — five tiers

5
Eye tracking — gaze heat maps & non-verbal access
Future

A front camera understands where a person is looking. This phase uses that to generate attention heat maps during exercises — showing which parts of the screen a client engaged with, which they avoided, and how that changes over time. Since clients may use the app at home on their own device (Android or iOS), eye tracking follows them there too. For clients who cannot communicate verbally or by touch, gaze becomes the interface.

Gaze heat maps overlaid on session exercises — see exactly where attention was focused
Hemispatial neglect detection — flag if client consistently ignores one screen half
Device calibration (one-time, ~2 min) — adapts to any Android or iOS hardware, stored locally
Client calibration (per session, 45 sec) — 5-dot sequence for individual gaze differences
Confidence scoring — low-quality calibration disables gaze selection and marks heat maps approximate
MediaPipe Iris — cross-platform, in-browser ML; iOS 18 native Eye Tracking activates as a higher-accuracy upgrade
Visual scanning exercise type — gaze-led, no touch required
Dwell-to-select: look at an option for 2 seconds to choose it (non-verbal clients)
Blink detection — 400–600ms threshold distinguishes deliberate from involuntary blinks
Large-target AAC-style interface — works on clinic tablet and client's home device
Gaze heat maps sync to database — visible in client profile regardless of which device was used
Android primary, iOS supported: MediaPipe Iris (Google open-source ML, runs in-browser via WebAssembly) works on any device with a front camera. On iOS 18+, the native Eye Tracking API activates automatically as a higher-accuracy layer. Because Headway centres use a variety of Android tablets and clients may use their own devices at home, a two-tier calibration system adapts to each device — device calibration (one-time, maps camera to screen) and client calibration (per session, accounts for sitting position and individual differences). Clinical-grade eye movement disorder diagnosis (Tobii hardware) is out of scope.

Why this matters beyond the app

Brain injury rehabilitation has a data problem. Outcomes are recorded on paper, in spreadsheets, or in proprietary hospital systems that don't talk to each other. There's no shared understanding of what "good recovery" looks like at three months, at six months, at two years.

If Headway deploys this tool across its network of centres, we will, for the first time, have a longitudinal, structured record of cognitive rehabilitation outcomes at scale in the UK. That's not just useful for key workers. It's useful for researchers, for commissioning bodies, and eventually for the NHS. And with eye tracking, we open the door to clients who currently have no voice in their own rehabilitation — because their eyes can tell us what their hands and words cannot.

Privacy first

Clients are identifiers, not names. No PII in the data layer. Each role sees only what they need — GDPR-compliant by design, not retrofit.

Clinician-led

AI suggests. The key worker decides. Always. Every recommendation has an override. The human is never bypassed.

Open data

Anonymised aggregate data should be shareable with researchers. Knowledge shouldn't be locked in one charity's database.
